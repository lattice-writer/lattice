# The source editor

One of the features [the GPUI app](../README.md) has so far.

A jump to a line (an error, the outline, SyncTeX, Go to line) flashes the whole line on the find-match colour, holds it and fades it over 1.6 s, as the Tauri app's `reveal-flash` does; under reduced motion it holds without fading; a jump into another editor takes the earlier editor's flash away at once.
The `attention` e2e module checks the flash comes and goes, and goes from the first editor when a jump lands in a second.

Each file's editor comes back where it was left, as the Tauri app keeps a text file's view state (`editor_panel/place_memory.rs`): its caret and its first line shown, per project, recorded at once as they move and written to `editor-places.json` in the app's state folder 250 ms after the last change (or as the editor closes or the app quits), so a file opened again in the same run or a later one returns to them.
A project keeps up to 200 files' places, the file touched longest ago going first; a rename or move, of the file or a folder holding it, open or not, carries a file's place to its new path, and an open at a line of its own (a search result, an outline entry, an error) goes to that line instead.
The `editor_place` e2e module checks a file closed with its caret and scroll deep in it opens again at the same caret and first line, opened at line 3 goes there, closed straight after a jump comes back to the jumped-to line, and renamed in the tree while closed opens under its new name where it was left.

Edits sources in Zed's editor with LaTeX highlighting in Lattice's code themes (and BibTeX for a bibliography and HTML for a `.html` file, its `<style>` as CSS and its `<script>` as JavaScript, as the Tauri app's codemirror-lang-bib and lang-html style them; a Markdown file's fenced `latex`, `bibtex`, `html`, `css` or `js` block is highlighted in its language), find and replace in the file ([Finding things](find.md)) and an optional Vim mode (Zed's Vim, with Lattice's own `:` command line at the editor's foot, where `:w` saves and builds as ⌘S does, `:q` closes the tab, `:wq` does both and `:N` goes to line N, as the Tauri app's CodeMirror Vim has them); the base keys where Zed's differ are CodeMirror's, as the Tauri app binds them (⌃L selects the line, recentering instead with the Emacs keymap on, ⌘I the enclosing syntax outside a `.tex` file, ⌥⌘\ re-indents, ⌃⌥[ and ⌃⌥] fold and unfold everything, and a ⌘-click adds a cursor; in a `.tex` file ⌃⌥[ and ⌥⌘[ fold the ranges the fold column folds, as the Tauri app's LaTeX fold service does); Format document (the palette's, in a `.tex` file) runs latexindent and applies its result only while the buffer still holds the text it was made from; A citation key's card shows what the Tauri card does beyond the bibliography: the library's author list, Crossref's word on the paper as a badge (Verified by Crossref, or the correction or retraction it reports, its tip saying who reported it and when), and "Cited N× here", a row per use of the key with the section it sits in, its line, the page its note names (which opens the paper there) and why it cites the paper, each row a jump to that use.
Its actions are Open paper (when the library has the paper's text or can download it, else the source its entry cites, in the browser also when the download fails; the title runs it too), Why cited, Go to entry and Copy key.
⌥⌘K opens the card for the key at the caret with its first action focused, as the Tauri app's does, Open paper showing ↩ for the Return that presses it.
Tab and ⇧Tab walk the actions (round from the last to the first), Escape gives the caret back, and an edit, a caret move or focus leaving the card closes it and gives the text the keyboard back; the pointer leaves it be, and with the key scrolled out of the editor ⌥⌘K opens none.
Why cited, from the card or a right-click on the key, files a citation reason on that use: one of five roles and an optional note, saved with the project's annotations; a reason follows its cite through edits, and one whose cite is gone, or that was resolved, joins the notes in the Annotations panel and the titlebar's count.
A reference card previews what its label names, as the Tauri app's does: a figure's image above it (a PDF figure's first page; an EPS says it has no preview), an equation set by KaTeX with the project's macros (its source until the picture is in, and when KaTeX cannot set it), and "(3), page 4 in the last build" from the last build's `.aux`; resting on an `\includegraphics` path opens a card of the figure alone.
Its Go to, on a hairline as the citation card's actions are, closes the card and opens the label where it is defined, its tooltip naming the ⌘-click that goes there directly.
In a command naming several labels (`\cref{a,b}`, `\Cref`) the card is the label under the pointer's alone, as the Tauri app's source card is, and a label nothing defines opens none (its lint card speaks for it); the one-section-per-label card is Visual mode's, on its chip, which the Visual LaTeX island draws with the Tauri app's own code.
The equations are KaTeX's own pictures, taken from the math island's page in a webview no window shows (about 250 ms for the first after a minute idle, a few milliseconds after that, and none for one already shown), so they match the Tauri app's cards pixel for pixel.
The cards (citation, reference, preview, math preview, and the PDF's citation and compound cards) are frosted as the Tauri app's are, 97% of the raised surface over the page blurred by 14px (`card_surface` in `latex/cards.rs`); a card over a native surface, such as a webview island like the Agent panel or a board, sheet or deck, is the solid surface instead, because Metal cannot read the island's pixels.
While the window holds a native surface, GPUI draws all deferred content, the cards among it, on a surface above the native views, and a card there blurs the page from the frame drawn under that surface.
In a `.tex` file the live math preview shows KaTeX's picture of the formula the caret is in, with the project's macros, on a card just above the formula's first line (below its last when there is no room, by the caret's line when the formula fills the editor), as the Tauri app's `math-preview.ts` does (`lattice_editor::latex::math_preview`, on the adapter's caret card, `caret_card.rs`).
The card is placed by the text's own box on those lines (the font's ascent and descent centred in the line, the box CodeMirror's `coordsAtPos` gives the Tauri card), from the size the preview reports before it is drawn, so it opens where it settles in its first frame; and an edit asks KaTeX for the new picture at once, rather than at the frame that draws the edit.
Typing updates the picture in place, one picture being set at a time, so a burst of keys ends on the last one's picture; half-typed math KaTeX cannot set keeps the last good picture, dimmed; an empty formula shows nothing.
The card takes no focus, shows only while the editor has the keyboard, hides when the caret leaves math, and Escape puts it away until the caret leaves that formula (an Escape that closes the completion menu is the menu's).
A picture taller than the card (240px, or 40% of the window) scrolls in it as far through as the caret is through the formula's source.
The source gutter is the Tauri app's: four-digit line numbers and a fold column whose chevrons show over the gutter and fold a LaTeX environment, section, preamble, brace group or comment block, so the text starts where CodeMirror's does.
A `\textcolor{c}{…}`'s text, and what a `\color{c}` declaration colours up to the end of its group (the `}` or `\end` that closes it), are drawn in their colour in the source and on the Visual page (lattice#1328), the innermost colour winning where they nest. A colour is one of xcolor's base names (`red`, `blue`, `teal`, …) or `[HTML]{RRGGBB}`; a mix such as `red!50`, another model or a `\definecolor` is left unpainted. So the text stays readable, its OKLab lightness is brought down to at most 0.50 in a light editor and up to at least 0.74 in a dark one, keeping hue and chroma, which holds every base colour at 4.5:1 or more on white and on #1E1E1E; a colour already that dark or light is drawn as written. Like the highlights, both views read only the lines in view and 2,000 characters either side (from the start of a line, and in one stretch where a fold's two sides meet), so a declaration further up than that is not followed. In a light editor a highlight's passage keeps the paper's ink over a colour, in both views.

A `.tex` file has the outline rail on the editor's right edge (its sections' marks, the one being read in the accent; resting on it opens the outline with numbers and PDF pages, and a row jumps there), and the Source / Visual switch in its strip (its ⋯ menu repeats it), one choice for every `.tex` file, shared by every window and remembered, as the Tauri app's is.
Visual is the Tauri app's Visual mode, its CodeMirror source editor drawn as a page (headings, emphasis, maths through KaTeX, lists, figures, tables, references and citations as chips, the preamble folded), as a webview island in the source's place over the panel's buffer: an edit lands in the file as the change it made and nothing else, undo and redo are the buffer's, so either view steps back through the other's edits, grouped as the page's own history groups them (a word typed in one go is one ⌘Z, as in the Tauri app, however its keystrokes reach the buffer), the caret is the status bar's and crosses the switch by line, a jump to a line lands in it, and a ⌘-click on a reference, citation or `\input` opens it. Its ⌘⇧E and ⌘⌥R ask for the name in the window's dialog, which the page then applies at its own selection, as the Tauri app's canvas does.
Find (⌘F and the strip's Find) opens the page's own search panel there, and an `\input` of an editable figure's entry file draws the figure.
The outline rail stands over the page's right edge as it does over the source (the Tauri app's own rail, its marks, the outline with numbers and PDF pages, and a row's jump showing the same place in the PDF).
TexLab's completion, hovers and Go to definition join its own when TexLab is installed, and without TexLab the labels and citation keys nothing uses are marked.
⇧F12 and F2 on a label or citation key open the window's references bar and rename dialog, and an image pasted there goes in as a figure at the page's caret, which the source's follows.
A citation chip's card there is the Tauri app's Visual card with what the source's card knows beyond the bibliography: the library's authors, Crossref's badge, "Cited N× here" with each use's section, page and reason (a row jumps to its use, a page opens the paper there), and Open paper, which opens what the source card's opens; as in the Tauri app, a chip's card offers no Why cited, which the source's card offers once the caret is in the chip, and a right-click on the key in the chip's source opens it too, saving or removing the reason in the project's annotations.
The project's local annotations are marked there as in the source, each passage tinted by its status with its numbered pin in the gutter; a pin opens its note in the Annotations panel, and the card over a passage resolves it or takes a reply. Co-authors' Overleaf carets are drawn there with their names.
In a linked Overleaf project the file's comment threads are marked there in their openers' colours, and margin review shows them beside the text as the source's does (the Tauri app's own layer: cards right of the text with room for the measure, else pips, as the palette's Margin review mode says), its cards replying, resolving, deleting and editing messages through the window's Overleaf bridge; Overleaf's tracked changes are marked there as in the source, with the same card under the pointer and their cards in margin review, Accept and Reject going to Overleaf through the bridge.
What it does not have yet is in [Not yet](not-yet.md).
It reads the project's citations and labels from the index the source's cards read, its TeX sources for macros and `\graphicspath`, its images through the island's asset protocol, and the last build's `.aux` for the numbers it draws.
